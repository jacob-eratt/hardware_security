\begin{abstract}
Cache replacement policies influence processor performance
and hardware security, but their implementations in commercial
processors are often undocumented. In this project, we
investigate whether neural sequence models can learn cache
replacement behavior from memory-access histories and eviction
outcomes. We implemented cache simulators and trained a Gated
Recurrent Unit (GRU) model to predict eviction victims under
tree-based pseudo-LRU (PLRU). Our initial experiments achieved
at least 99.988\% test accuracy across 4-, 8-, and 16-way
simulated caches. On histories that include misses, it
reached 96.1\% on the adaptive DRRIP policy and 73.6\% on
PLRU, with accuracy decreasing as misses increased.
Future work will evaluate generalization and extend the
approach to real Intel processors, starting with the L2
cache of an Intel Celeron N3450, to characterize undocumented
replacement policies.
\end{abstract}
